Titan Interior MCMC Models: Summary & Results

This page summarizes the various MCMC modeling efforts for Titan's interior, focusing on the trade-offs between ocean-bearing and ocean-free structures and their ability to match Cassini tidal observations.

Test 41: Andrade No-Ocean

No Ocean Andrade Rheology

A 5D MCMC exploration of a fully solid Titan (450 km hydrosphere). Investigates if Ice Ih dissipation alone can match observations without the decoupling effect of an ocean.

Key Finding: Matches Re(k₂) ≈ 0.61. Heating is concentrated in Ice Ih (60-70%) due to the thin stagnant lid provided by a 4 km clathrate cap.

Test 42: Maxwell Ocean

Ocean Maxwell Rheology

A 5D exploration with a 28 km subsurface ocean. Uses the classical Maxwell viscoelastic model.

Key Finding: Matches Re(k₂) but underpredicts Im(k₂) by 10-20%. Heating is dominated by HP ice dissipation (~70-80% Ice VI).

Test 46: Andrade Hybrid-Hydro

Ocean Variable Structure DS2001 Convection

A 10D MCMC allowing both rheology and structure (D_hydro, Tb) to vary. Uses the Deschamps & Sotin (2001) Cartesian convection scaling.

Key Finding: Found that DS2001 scaling law underestimates heat flux for thick shells, leading to very thin stagnant lids (~6-10 km).

Test 48: Andrade Yao2014 (Path B)

Ocean Yao 2014 Spherical

Refines Test 46 by using spherical-shell convection scaling (Yao et al. 2014). This produces thicker, more realistic stagnant lids (~33-45 km).

Key Finding: Titan tidal heating is dominated by warm basal Ice Ih. The observed |Im(k₂)| ≈ 0.135 is at the edge of reachable values for ocean-bearing models due to HP ice decoupling.

Test 50: Andrade No-Ocean + Yao 2014

No Ocean Yao 2014 8D MCMC

Specifically tests Petricca et al. (2025) hypothesis: a no-ocean Titan where HP ices can dissipate efficiently. Samples the no-ocean branch cleanly by marginalizing over basal temperature.

Key Finding: Successfully explains high Im(k₂) via HP ice dissipation (Ice VI), provided η_HP is low (~10¹² Pa·s). Silicate viscosity constrained to ~10¹⁹·⁶ Pa·s.
